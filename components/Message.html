Why this exists

I love rewriting. Write down a few rules that swap one thing for another, apply them over and over, and shapes turn up that nobody drew.

Rewriting goes back to Axel Thue, who asked in 1914 which strings of symbols a fixed set of substitutions can turn into which (English translation). In 1969 John Pfaltz and Azriel Rosenfeld carried the idea from strings to labelled graphs, which they called webs, and gave us graph grammars. Wave function collapse by Maxim Gumin learns which pieces may sit next to each other from a single example, then fills a grid with output that only ever follows what it learned.

This site borrows from all three. A–Z is the example: every line a new letter draws has to sit somewhere A–Z already uses. A grammar rewrites a glyph into parts and its parts into pieces, joint rules settle where the parts meet, and anything that breaks a rule is thrown away and drawn again.
